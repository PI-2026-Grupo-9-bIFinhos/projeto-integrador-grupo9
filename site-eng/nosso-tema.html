Health and Nutrition: The Science of Well-Being

A healthy diet is the fundamental basis for sustaining life, preventing disease, and promoting longevity. Far beyond merely satisfying hunger, proper nutrition provides the macronutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, and fats) and micronutrients (vitamins and minerals) essential for cellular function, hormonal regulation, and strengthening the immune system.

Toledo, Paraná – Health and Nutrition

The state of nutrition and health in Toledo is excellent, making the city a standout example in Brazil. Unlike many other places—where healthcare focuses only on those who are already ill—Toledo has a robust network that leverages high-quality food to prevent people from becoming sick in the first place.

Toledo manages to provide high-quality food—either at a very low cost or for free—through its eight public restaurants and community gardens, while leveraging the public healthcare system (SUS) infrastructure to teach residents how to eat better.

Municipal Initiatives:

  • Toledo Means Greater Food Security!: Launched in October 2022, this project brings nutritionists directly to the people. Teams visit the public restaurants to offer tips on healthy meal choices, check weights, and provide a brief nutritional assessment for those dining there. At local health units (UBSs), they distribute seasoned salt (to help manage high blood pressure) and informational pamphlets. All of this is part of the Municipal Food and Nutritional Security Plan, which was developed in April 2016.
  • Food Bank: Opened in April 2024 in the Jardim São Francisco neighborhood, this initiative combats food waste by collecting surplus, high-quality food from local businesses and farms and redistributing it to those in need. It also features a "Teaching Kitchen" for sharing nutritious recipes and employs a dehydration technique that extends the shelf life of food without compromising its nutritional value.
  • Top-Tier School Meals and "School for Parents": Meals provided in municipal schools and daycare centers (CMEIs) are strictly overseen by nutritionists from the Department of Education. All school meals are based on "real food"—sourced directly from small-scale local producers through incentive policies that have been in place since 2014. The current focus is on initiatives involving families, engaging parents through talks so that the good habits cultivated at school are continued at home.
