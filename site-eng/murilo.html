About Us

Murilo:

Birth:

My life's journey began fifteen years ago, when my mother discovered she was pregnant. The news brought a sense of great anticipation to the entire family, who waited eagerly for my arrival. On April 4, 2011, I entered the world. As the eldest son, first grandson, and first great-grandson, my arrival was an unforgettable milestone for every generation of the family.

What happened on the day I was born:

On April 4, 2011, U.S. President Barack Obama officially announced the start of his reelection campaign, and the company TEPCO began dumping 11,500 tons of radioactive water into the Pacific Ocean to free up storage space for emergency teams in Japan.

Educational Journey:

It all began when my mother—well before I was born—started a teacher-training program in high school. She worked in daycare centers, so when I was born, she didn’t think twice; that was the start of my own educational journey.

At age four, my mother began working at the La Salle school, and—as is often the case for a teacher’s child—I attended that same school. From ages six to seven, I attended the Incomar school for the same reason. At age eight, due to financial circumstances, I attended a public school called Egon Werner Bercht. From ages nine to eleven, I attended the Integral school, having gained admission by passing an entrance exam.

From the age of twelve to fourteen, I attended the Alfa school. Now, at fifteen, I am a student at the Federal Institute of Paraná (IFPR), having been admitted through a competitive selection process.

Current Situation

Studying at the IFPR, I receive a high-quality education. I am very happy to be a student at this institute, and I have already gained a clear idea of ​​which field I would like to pursue.

Future Prospects:

My goal is to complete my high school education at the IFPR, and I intend to pursue a degree in a field that combines technology with teaching (licentiate studies).


Where We Live:

Municipality:

I reside in the city of Toledo, Paraná, which has an estimated population of 162,658 inhabitants, according to data from the IBGE (Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics). Before the city was founded, the area served as a trail for drovers and *yerba mate* harvesters. One of their campsites and resting spots was known as "Pouso Toledo." Toledo hosts the famous *Festa Nacional do Porco no Rolete* (National Whole-Roasted Pig Festival)—a gastronomic event with over 45 years of tradition and international recognition that draws thousands of visitors to the city. Toledo is a benchmark for infrastructure, boasting an extensive network of paved rural roads and an agricultural GDP that places it at the top of national municipal development rankings.

Neighborhood:

Within the city, I live in the Centro (Downtown) neighborhood. It is one of the most dynamic, highly valued, and well-equipped areas of the city, serving as its financial hub. It houses major public institutions, the largest banking chains, restaurants, and traditional cultural venues, and it experiences the highest daily foot traffic in the region.

One of the key landmarks in the central area is the Christ the King Cathedral (Catedral Cristo Rei)—an imposing religious and visual icon of the city. Located near Largo São Vicente de Paulo, it stands close to the municipality’s original, pioneering avenues, such as the historic Avenida Maripá.
