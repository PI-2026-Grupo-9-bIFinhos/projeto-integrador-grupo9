Health through nutrition

Students : Murilo Dalla Barba Temporini e Pedro Miguel Kliemann

Diet that causes harm

It was an ordinary day in the city of São Paulo, where a family—a father, a mother, and their 17-year-old son—went about their daily routine. The son had always relied entirely on his mother; he got everything he wanted, and whenever he asked for something, she would provide it. He would often ask her to cook various dishes, though she always made sure to include foods that would support his health. A year later, the son turned 18 and saw an opportunity to move out and live on his own. As she helped him pack, his mother reminded him to keep taking care of his diet. In the first few days, he paid no mind to her advice; he spent all his money on high-calorie, non-nutritious foods and gained a significant amount of weight. Realizing he needed a change, he went to the grocery store and bought various products labeled as "fit," "light," or "calorie-free." After a week of eating these items, he felt lethargic and began to fall ill. He went to his mother’s house, where she explained that such products wouldn't improve his health—what he needed instead were wholesome, nutrient-rich meals. Following her guidance, he bought staples like rice, beans, salad, and meat. By paying closer attention to his nutrition, he lost weight and regained his health. It was then that he learned his lesson: a healthy diet is one that contains a variety of macronutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, and fats) and micronutrients (vitamins and minerals)—not just foods that are marketed as "good" for you.
